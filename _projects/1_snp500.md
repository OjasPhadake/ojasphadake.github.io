**[Code on GitHub](https://github.com/OjasPhadake/snp500)** · Python · FastAPI · SQLite/PostgreSQL

**Problem.** Backtests run on today's S&P 500 list quietly drop every company that went bankrupt, was acquired or got removed. That survivorship bias inflates returns. To avoid it you need to know who was _actually_ in the index on any given day, under the ticker and sector it had then.

**What I built.** An event-sourced database that reconstructs 25+ years of membership. `get_constituents("2008-09-15")` returns the 499 companies in the index that morning, including Lehman Brothers, Washington Mutual and Fannie Mae.

- **Entity resolution:** a persistent company ID is kept separate from tickers and names. This handles renames, ticker re-use, re-entries and acquirers that take the target's name.
- **Provenance:** every event cites its source URL, fetch time and the SHA-256 of the raw bytes. The rebuild is offline and deterministic.
- **Explicit disagreement handling:** conflicting sources are reconciled and reported, never silently overwritten.
- FastAPI endpoints and a dashboard with Kaplan–Meier survival curves of index membership. 29 scenario tests cover the hard cases.

**Result.** 1,182 companies, 1,982 events and 1,194 membership intervals. No company is missing against the reference dataset on any compared date from 2001 to 2026.
